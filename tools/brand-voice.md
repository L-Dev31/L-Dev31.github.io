# Léo Tosku — positioning and voice

Written from the site itself, the public web (the portfolio is the only indexed page about you; GitHub bio "3D modelling / Web design / Programming / Modding", 11 repositories, Nice) and from how you write when you brief the work.

## Who the visitor meets

A designer who also builds. One person from the first sketch to the deployed page, so nothing is lost between design and code. Proof is odd and specific rather than polished: a regional flag tested in wind simulations, a binary editor for a Nintendo text format, a Creole translator, a brochure made in 48 hours for a jury, a marketplace founded at 17, marketing and design for a superyacht engineering firm in Monaco.

Two audiences, one page:
- **Employers and clients** scan for role, proof and an easy way to start. They give a portfolio seconds, not minutes.
- **Young collaborators** scan for taste. Culture (Ghibli, Nolan, Nintendo, Wii, Undertale, Omori, Iron Lung) is the signal that you think like them.

## Voice

- First person, short sentences, concrete nouns. Say what was done, for whom, how long it took.
- Calm and factual. No "passionate", "creative", "unique", no exclamation marks beyond the greeting.
- A dry line is allowed when it is true ("I'll ship it all beautifully.", "breaking websites since 10").
- Numbers over adjectives: 17, 48 hours, 50+ pieces, 955/990.
- Section notes keep three lines. Body copy stays under about 70 words per block.
- Decisive curation is part of the voice: fewer things, chosen, and an honest "see all" for the rest (Culture does this).

## Marketing rules applied to a portfolio (a poster for your services)

1. **Above the fold says who, what and for whom** in a few seconds: motto, "Design | Dev | Marketing", and a line that names the job (brands and interfaces, built by me).
2. **One primary action.** "Start a project" is now the hero button and the closing link of "What you get"; the footer repeats it with the coffee invitation, the mail, the number and Discord. Everything else is secondary.
3. **Fewer, deeper projects first.** The home showcase holds five (Black Goose, Proofy, Charlie McKenzie, BMG Editor, Saint-Martin Flag), chosen for range: web, product UX, development, one original piece. All eight stay in the index.
4. **Case studies answer problem, process, outcome** and carry the role and year at the top, as the redesigned project pages do.
5. **Proof next to the claim.** Facts live in the copy (marketplace at 17, 50+ pieces at Red Planet Pictures, TOEIC 955/990, NSI trophy) instead of a testimonials block at the bottom.
6. **Taste is shown, not announced.** Culture is trimmed to the works that say something, with a plain list for the rest.
7. **Treat the site as a product:** fast, readable on a phone, no dead links, honest metadata (titles and descriptions now say what you do and that you are open to projects).

## Still open (needs your material, not just wording)

- A real result or quote per project (a client sentence, a number, a screenshot of it live). It is the strongest thing a poster can add.
- A short "what I take on" list with a format and a lead time (brand identity, website, app UX) so a client knows what to ask for.
- The only thing the web knows about you is this site. A filled LinkedIn headline and a GitHub profile README pointing here would double the footprint. The GitHub bio mentions 3D modelling and modding; the portfolio now mentions modding, not yet 3D.
- The McDonald's line is the one search engines quoted from the About page. The About description now leads with the work instead.
