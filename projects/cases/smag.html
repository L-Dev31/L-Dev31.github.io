The Brief

Super Mario Another Galaxy is an unofficial mod of Super Mario Galaxy 2. I started it in 2020 and it has been developed ever since by a team of eleven people in seven countries. It adds ten new galaxies, an original soundtrack, a new story and a new observatory, and it runs on the American, European, Japanese, Korean and Taiwanese versions of the game.

I lead the project. I model the planets, design levels with Solargress, write the dialogue and translations with him, and I built the official website.

Léo Tosku (project lead, 3D art, level design, website) · Solargress (music, system, level creation) · Super Hackio (programming) · eight more contributors in 2D art, 3D art and technical help

Every expert was once a beginner.

The Project

A Sequel That Plays Like the Original

The goal is simple to state and slow to reach: new galaxies that could pass for Nintendo’s own. Super Hackio, our programmer, made the Galaxy Level Engine, which the project credits, and handles all of its programming.

Five years in, the mod is still unfinished. I put that down to my perfectionism: I refuse to ship a model I would not call perfect in every way. It slows the schedule, and it is also why the models hold up.

The Process

1. Learning 3D From Nothing

My first tests were cubes in SketchUp. Then a tutorial by AlexSMG on importing models into the game showed me Blender, and my first real model there is what made me want to start SMAG.

For a long time I copied planets from the official games to understand how they are built, then borrowed assets from other models until I could draw my own. It took five years to reach models polished enough that someone who has not played the games in a long time could take them for official ones.

2. A Beach From Home

Coconut Beach Galaxy is the level I wanted to be personal. As a kid I found hidden lakes and wild beaches in the Var around Le Muy, and on my island, Guadeloupe. I wanted a player to feel that same surprise.

It started as a pencil sketch, went through several rebuilds, and is now finished, with both stars complete.

3. Light in the Vertices

The jump in quality came when I started giving real care to vertex shading, painting light and shadow into the model itself. The same planet looks flat and dark in the first version and finished in the second.

4. Eleven People, Seven Countries

Solargress composes the music and manages the systems. Super Hackio programs. Gareth48, Hollow and Tetracøld work on 2D art and textures, Mixlas and AlexSMG on 3D art, Zyphron.G on skyboxes, and Floaty and Louis Miles on levels and models. The team talks on Discord, and every galaxy has a public progress bar on the website so everyone sees what remains.

The Design

5. Five Years of Logo

The logo grew with our skills. The first, in 2020, was made in Pixellab on an Android phone. Solargress redid it in Photoshop in 2021. The 2022 version, my first 3D logo in Blender, was the one that lasted the shortest time. In 2023 it was rebuilt closer to a classic Mario Galaxy logo, and the 2025 one is rendered in Blender, inspired by the Super Mario Galaxy movie.

Three rules held throughout: stay close to the game’s identity but feel like a step up, stay readable at any size, and get as near to Nintendo’s quality as possible.

6. The Official Website

I built the site in plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript. It has a screenshot carousel, a player for each track of the soundtrack, the latest videos, a blog with search, a progress page with a bar for every star of every galaxy, and credits with a card for each person that shows their country.

All of it is read from JSON files, so the team updates the music, posts and progress without touching the layout.

The canceled models weren’t failures, they were stepping stones.

The Outcome

After five years, the mod is still in development. On the progress page, Coconut Beach Galaxy is complete, Pollen Highlands Galaxy has its first star done, Factory Fortress Galaxy is at 70 percent and the main hub at 80.

I came out of it with 3D modeling and level design, but also English, communication, team management and web development, all learned because the project needed them.
