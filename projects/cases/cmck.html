The Brief

Charlie McKenzie composes music for films and retro-style games. We are actually close friends and have collaborated on a bunch of projects over the years. He had already built a great YouTube audience around the nostalgic sound of 2000s Nintendo and indie games, but he needed a proper portfolio to turn those casual listeners into paying clients.

He asked me to build the site from scratch, covering the concept, copy, and frontend. The goal was straightforward: make it feel nostalgic yet completely fresh, without leaning on cheap retro clichés.

Because we have collaborated for years, I know his sound inside out. The job wasn't to just slap pixel fonts on a page. It was to build a site that looked exactly the way his music feels.

The Problem

A Shared Vision

Most retro websites rely on generic 80s arcade tropes. But Charlie’s music is rooted in the 2000s handheld era and modern indie games. The design needed to reflect that specific, cleaner aesthetic.

Since we have worked together on so many projects over the years, we skipped the usual formal client discovery phase. We already knew what the site had to be: a space that felt nostalgic enough for his YouTube fans, but professional and polished enough to pitch cinematic film scores to directors.

The Process

1. Modern Indie Meets 2000s Nostalgia

We went with a clean, monochromatic direction. The layout uses modern web spacing, but the typography gives it that crisp, pixel-perfect vibe of an early 2000s handheld interface.

I restricted the color palette heavily, only using gradients to highlight key interactive elements. This keeps the focus entirely on the audio. It ensures the site feels appropriate whether you are listening to an upbeat Nintendo remix or a dark, atmospheric film soundtrack.

2. Tactile UI & Screen Textures

To give the site some digital texture, I added very subtle screen effects like faint scanlines and a slight chromatic aberration on the text. It hints at older digital screens without making the site hard to read.

The interactive elements are completely tactile. When you click a button, it physically depresses down and to the right while the drop shadow shrinks. It is a direct nod to the chunky plastic buttons of early 2000s consoles.

3. Serving Multiple Audiences

The site has to speak to completely different people. The portfolio splits his work into clear categories: personal YouTube projects, indie game scores, and film music commissions. Visitors can immediately filter for what they actually care about from the first scroll.

I also structured the copy to preemptively answer the questions a game developer or film director will have. We covered turnaround times and mixing quality right before the contact form to remove any friction from reaching out.

The Result

Building a site for a friend and frequent collaborator meant the vision was locked in from day one. The final result is a custom portfolio where every detail, from the tactile UI buttons to the crisp typography, feels completely true to his sound. It captures the specific nostalgia of 2000s gaming and the polish of modern indie titles, while doing the heavy lifting of turning casual listeners into paying clients.
