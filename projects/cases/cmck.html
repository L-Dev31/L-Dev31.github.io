The Brief

Charlie McKenzie composes music for films and retro-style games. We are close friends and have worked together on many projects. He had built a strong YouTube audience around the nostalgic sound of 2000s Nintendo and indie games, and needed a portfolio to turn listeners into paying clients.

He asked me to build the site, from the concept and copy to the front end. The goal was a site that feels nostalgic and fresh at once, without cheap retro clichés.

After years of collaboration, I know his sound well. The job was to build a site that looks the way his music feels, which takes more than pixel fonts.

The Problem

A Shared Vision

Most retro websites rely on generic 80s arcade tropes. Charlie's music comes from the 2000s handheld era and modern indie games, so the design had to reflect that cleaner aesthetic.

Since we have worked together for years, we skipped the usual client discovery phase. We already knew what the site had to be: nostalgic enough for his YouTube fans, polished enough to pitch film scores to directors.

The Process

1. Modern Indie Meets 2000s Nostalgia

We chose a clean, monochromatic direction. The layout uses modern web spacing, and the typography brings the crisp, pixel-perfect feel of an early 2000s handheld interface.

I kept the palette very restricted and used gradients only to highlight interactive elements. This keeps the focus on the audio, whether it's an upbeat Nintendo remix or a dark film soundtrack.

2. Tactile UI & Screen Textures

For digital texture, I added subtle screen effects: faint scanlines and a slight chromatic aberration on the text. They hint at older screens without hurting readability.

Buttons are tactile. On click, they press down and to the right while the drop shadow shrinks, a nod to the chunky plastic buttons of early 2000s consoles.

3. Serving Multiple Audiences

The site speaks to different audiences. The portfolio splits his work into clear categories: personal YouTube projects, indie game scores and film commissions, so visitors can filter for what they care about from the first scroll.

I also wrote the copy to answer the questions a game developer or a film director will have. Turnaround times and mixing quality appear right before the contact form, to remove friction from getting in touch.

The Outcome

Building a site for a friend and regular collaborator meant the vision was clear from day one. The result is a custom portfolio where every detail, from the tactile buttons to the typography, stays true to his sound, and that turns casual listeners into paying clients.
