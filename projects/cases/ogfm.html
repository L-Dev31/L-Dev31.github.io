The Brief

OG FM is an underground French webradio, little known by design. Their motto: « Pour les jeunes cons qui sont devenus vieux. » For the young fools who grew old. A station for people who refuse to let the music they loved quietly disappear.

For their 30th anniversary, they needed something to match that energy: a complete brand identity system (logo mark and logotype, brand charter, 3 print executions, 1 social media asset), for a station with almost no visual presence and a very niche, nostalgia-driven audience. The identity had to feel urgent, physical and unapologetic, and could not be mistaken for any other webradio.

A punk identity still needs a system, and this one follows its own rules.

The Problem

A Brand Without a Face

Their original logo looked like any other webradio: generic, balanced, forgettable. For a station built on cultural defiance, the visual identity contradicted everything else. OG FM had a strong voice and almost no visual presence. The new identity had to be recognizable at once, hard to confuse with anyone else, and solid across every format: social media, posters, print and anniversary materials.

The Process

1. Strategic Foundation: Aesthetic Direction

Research pointed to UK DIY and fanzine culture. That was the right direction, with one problem: most contemporary brands borrow that aesthetic in Photoshop, and it shows. The question was how to make the result feel earned rather than borrowed. If the process was authentic, the output could not be faked.

2. Logo Development: Analog-First Execution

The logo started with a ballpoint pen and a sheet of aged paper, with no grid and no undo, so every mark stayed where it landed. After scanning, I vectorized the trembling lines in Illustrator as they were and kept the irregularities, since a vector tool would have smoothed them out.

3. Type System & Brand Charter

Seven typefaces, each chosen for a different era: punk, 80s rock, DIY fanzines, contemporary underground. Together they tell the history of OG FM, which is why I chose them.

The system builds new rules instead: size hierarchies, spacing ratios, stretch and compress parameters that make seven competing fonts coexist. It held across 30+ applications (posters, social posts, print) without any two assets looking like copies.

The brand charter locks it in: logo variations (horizontal, stacked, icon-only), a 5-color palette, the full type system, the spacing grid and the usage guidelines.

4. Campaign Execution & Deliverables

Five deliverables for the 30th anniversary campaign: an Instagram post, an A2 poster, an A4 flyer, a merchandise label and a 12-page brand charter. Each has a different format and follows the same rules. The test of an identity system is whether its rules hold across every format. Logo, typography, color and grid all held, and the rules guided every decision without making two pieces look alike.

The identity had to be recognizable first, and hard to ignore.

The Outcome

First complete brand identity, first graphic charter. Built solo, from brief to final assets in Adobe Illustrator: logo system, type system, 5-color palette, 3 print executions, 1 social media asset and a 12-page brand charter.

What I took from it: a design system makes every later decision faster and cleaner.
