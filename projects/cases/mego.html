The Brief

On 19–20 February 2026, Sophia Ynov Campus ran a 48-hour challenge for Bachelor 1 and 2 students in the Digital Project Management track. The brief: pitch a real commercial solution to a real client in 48 hours, in front of a jury. Our client was Mégoteur, a French startup with a GPS-enabled backpack vacuum that collects cigarette butts at scale, aimed at municipalities and public bodies.

The deliverables were fixed: a 2-page BtoG commercial brochure, presented in 10 minutes before the jury. We also made an Instagram post kit as the mandatory bonus. I was the only designer on the team, in charge of every visual: the brochure layout and the Instagram kit.

Léo Tosku (design) · Camilia Yadel (research & content) · Benoîte Macchi (research & presentation) · Lyna Djian (research & presentation)

142 billion cigarette butts are discarded every year in France. The problem was documented and the solution existed. It needed a face.

The Problem

A Strong Product, a Weak Pitch Window

Mégoteur had a solid case: GPS-tracked collection, ALCOME state funding, circular valorization of butts into urban furniture. But the target was municipalities, over-solicited decision-makers with at most 10 minutes and 3 pages to give. The design challenge was to land a complex BtoG value proposition before attention ran out.

The Process

1. BtoG Brochure (Léo Tosku, based on research by Camilia Yadel)

Camilia mapped the key arguments: the ALCOME funding angle, GPS traceability, the circular economy story and the cost per commune. My job was to compress them into 3 pages a municipal director would finish reading. Every layout choice served legibility under time pressure: a clear hierarchy, no decoration competing with the message, and a structure the eye can follow without instruction.

2. Instagram Kit (Léo Tosku)

The bonus deliverable was a municipality-facing Instagram carousel, designed to stop the scroll and make the case in four slides: a full-bleed shock image with a dark gradient and the Mégoteur logo at top left, a stat slide with the impact number, an explanatory slide on the problem and solution, and a closing message.

The design language is contrast: dark backgrounds, white body text, and cyan highlight blocks on the words that need to land. It is the same primary color as across all the Mégoteur deliverables, chosen to read as urgent without turning aggressive.

Two deliverables in 48 hours, under real constraints.

The Outcome

A complete visual communication package for a real BtoG client, delivered in competition conditions: a 2-page commercial brochure and a municipality-facing Instagram kit. The team placed second out of five, with a 12/20 on strict jury criteria.

What I took from it: designing under hard time pressure, with real constraints and a non-design audience, is a different skill from designing freely. Speed forces priorities, and every element that doesn't earn its place gets cut.
